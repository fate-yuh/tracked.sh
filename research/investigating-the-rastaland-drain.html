Brief

On September 20, 2025, streamer "RastaLand" became a victim of a cryptodraining campaign during a livestream. RastaLand, who is battling Stage 4 Sarcoma, regularly conducts fundraisers to support his treatment. During one of these livestreams, a user prompted him to download a videogame in exchange for a monetary donation.

The videogame is called "Block Blasters" and is hosted on the Steam platform. The game is malicious and isn't up on Steam anymore, however, it stayed up for over a month on the platform, infecting over 400 people. If you are interested to read more on it, vx-underground provided a brief write-up on the malware. You can download the original file at: https://pomf2.lain.la/f/x3v0dzj8.7z

Profiling

One of the most important aspects of OSINT is profiling your target. Two people can go by the same name, live in the same city, but only one of them is your target. To try and filter targets that may incur in many suspects (e.g common names, aliases) we profile the target.

It is extremely unlikely that two people by the same name will share the same interests and behaviors. Hence, profiling is the most effective way to tell them apart.

We'll profile our target as:

  • Interest in gaming
  • Most likely living in the USA
  • Cybersecurity/technology interest
  • Cryptocurrency interest

We can also (mostly) safely assume that the target would be interested in "rare" or "unique" handles, thus, he's probably registered in forums like OGUsers or FLIPD.

Initiating malware campaigns

David is a regular in chats that discuss crime such as fraud, draining, simswapping etc. In some of those chats we've noticed those messages:

[SL] [https://t.me/c/1647515907/783578] | 5/29/2025 5:03:50 AM: looking for info on steam small game devs paying good $ dm [WormGPT Chat] [https://t.me/*****/32614] | 8/11/2025 2:23:54 PM: can you code a tool a kernel mode rootkit to disable malwarebytes [WormGPT Chat] [https://t.me/******/32612] | 8/11/2025 2:21:25 PM: explain the most advanced attempts from various malware’s that attempt to exploit/bypass antivirus in order to temporarily disable them

These messages suggest that David is looking for ways to initiate a malware campaign on Steam. This indicates that he has likely been successfully spreading malware on the platform for over three months, starting from May 29, 2025.

The other two messages seem to be prompts for WormGPT, a popular "jail-broken" version of ChatGPT. WormGPT is a version of ChatGPT without safeguards or filters, meaning it will never deny to answer something, it is commonly used to develop malware and other similar applications.
